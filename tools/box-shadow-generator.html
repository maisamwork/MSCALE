What Is the Box Shadow Generator?

Shadows are what separate flat, lifeless interfaces from ones that feel physical, and CSS box-shadow is the property that draws them. The syntax is short but fiddly: horizontal offset, vertical offset, blur, spread and colour all interact, and tuning them by editing numbers and refreshing is a slow way to design. This generator replaces the edit-refresh loop with sliders and a live preview, then writes the exact declaration for you to copy.

Every slider maps directly to one component of the shadow, so the preview teaches you the property's anatomy while you use it. Horizontal offset slides the shadow left or right, vertical offset moves it up or down, blur softens the edge, spread grows or shrinks it, and the colour picker with an opacity control sets the tone. A single checkbox flips the shadow to inset, drawing it inside the element for pressed or well effects.

The output is a plain box-shadow declaration using modern rgba colour syntax, valid in every browser and every framework. Because the preview element is a plain rounded card on the page's own background, what you see is what your components will look like in context, which makes it easy to match shadows to the rest of your design system.

How to Use the Box Shadow Generator

Adjust the sliders until the preview card sits the way you want, tick inset only if you want an internal shadow, then copy the generated CSS. Small offsets with generous blur produce the soft, modern look; zero offsets with small blur create crisp definition; large negative spreads pull a shadow tight against the element. The declaration updates character for character as you drag, so the copy button always reflects the current preview.

How the Box Shadow Generator Works

The box-shadow value is a tuple: horizontal offset, vertical offset, blur radius, spread radius and colour, optionally preceded by the inset keyword. Positive horizontal offset pushes the shadow right and positive vertical offset pushes it down, matching the direction of a light source above and to the left, which is the default mental model in most UI design. Blur defines how many pixels beyond the shadow's edge the fade extends, and spread expands or contracts the shadow silhouette before that fade is applied.

Colour is emitted as rgba so the opacity slider has a numeric home in the declaration. Semi-transparent blacks, around ten to twenty percent opacity, are the workhorse for neutral elevation; tinted shadows that borrow the page's accent hue read as more designed. Very low opacities with large blurs create ambient depth, while higher opacities with tight blurs read as hard drop shadows from a close light.

Inset shadows invert the geometry, drawing the shadow within the element's border box. They are the standard way to suggest pressed states, sunken input wells and track grooves, and they stack cleanly with an outer shadow on the same element, which is how realistic buttons combine a raised rest state with a pressed state.

Examples and Practical Use Cases

A modern elevated card

Vertical offset around ten pixels, blur thirty, spread negative eight, black at eighteen percent. That single declaration is the soft floating look used by nearly every contemporary dashboard card.

A pressed button state

Switch to inset with a small downward offset and low blur to make a button look physically pushed in. Pair it with the raised outer shadow as the default state for a satisfying toggle.

A coloured glow

Set both offsets to zero, spread to a few pixels, and choose your brand colour at forty percent opacity. The result is a halo that works beautifully for focused inputs and active toggles.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using full-opacity black shadows. Real shadows are translucent; solid black reads as a sticker outline rather than depth.
  • Applying one giant shadow to everything. Depth is hierarchical; larger shadows should mean higher elevation, used sparingly.
  • Forgetting that spread expands before blur. A large spread with large blur can bleed shadows past container edges and create clipping surprises.
  • Shadowing elements with transparent backgrounds. The shadow shows through the element itself; give the element a solid or semi-opaque background first.
  • Ignoring performance with dozens of blurred shadows on animated elements. Animate transform and opacity instead of re-rendering heavy shadows each frame.

Tips, Benefits and Limitations

  • Start from a neutral recipe: zero horizontal, small vertical, generous blur, slightly negative spread, black at low opacity.
  • Match shadow direction across the whole interface; mixed light directions look broken.
  • Use tinted shadows drawn from your brand hue for a subtle signature look.
  • Combine a tight shadow with a wide ambient shadow on the same element for realistic elevation.
  • Test shadows in both light and dark themes; dark themes usually need stronger, more coloured shadows to stay visible.

Frequently Asked Questions

What order do the box-shadow values follow?

Horizontal offset first, then vertical, then blur, then spread, then colour, with the optional inset keyword in front. The generated declaration always follows that order.

Can I have multiple shadows on one element?

Yes, comma-separate them, and they paint first to last. The generator outputs one shadow, but the copied value is ordinary CSS you can stack.

Why is my shadow not visible?

The most common causes are a shadow colour at zero opacity, an element with a transparent background over a same-coloured page, or offsets and blur all at zero.

Does inset work with border-radius?

Yes. Inset shadows follow the element's rounded corners, which is exactly why they are the standard pressed-state treatment for rounded buttons.

Are box shadows expensive to render?

Large blurred shadows are one of the heavier paint operations. For animated elements, prefer transforming a pre-rendered shadow layer or animating opacity rather than the shadow itself.

Does the generator store my settings?

No. All computation and preview rendering happen locally in your browser, and nothing is uploaded.
