How the Fuel Cost Calculator Works

The Fuel Cost Calculator implements Cost = (distance / 100) × consumption per 100 km × price per liter, which is the standard relationship used in textbooks, reference tables and professional practice for this exact question. The page does not improvise a shortcut or a rounded constant — it performs the same operation a careful person would do with a calculator and a formula sheet, just without the slips.

Precision is handled deliberately. JavaScript computes with 64-bit double-precision floating point, so intermediate values keep about 15 significant digits and only the displayed result is rounded. That ordering matters: rounding early is the classic way small errors become large ones, and the tool avoids it by construction.

Because the calculation is local, the tool is also safe for figures you would rather not paste into an unknown website — salaries, invoices, grades, medical numbers. Nothing is transmitted, nothing is logged, and closing the tab clears every field. The result is deterministic too: the same inputs always produce the same output, so you can reproduce any figure you quote later.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Leaving a field blank or typing text into a number input. The calculator reports incomplete inputs rather than guessing, so fill every field before pressing Calculate.
  • Mixing units across inputs — for example entering one value in centimeters and another in meters. Convert everything to a single unit first and the formula takes care of the rest.
  • Efficiency figures from the brochure assume ideal conditions; city driving commonly uses 15–30% more fuel than the rating suggests.
  • Rounding intermediate results when chaining calculations. Keep full precision until the final step, then round once.
  • Forgetting that the output is not saved. A refresh resets the form, so copy any figure you need into your notes before leaving the page.
