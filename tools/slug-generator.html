What Is the Slug Generator?

A slug is the human-readable, URL-safe tail of a web address, like how-to-bake-sourdough in a blog post URL. Good slugs are short, lowercase, hyphen-separated and descriptive, and they help both readers and search engines understand a link before clicking it. This Slug Generator converts any title or phrase into a clean slug instantly, with batch mode for whole lists.

Type or paste a title and the tool strips accents and special characters, lowercases, replaces spaces with your chosen separator, and trims the result. Paste one title per line to convert an entire batch at once, which is how you keep the URLs of a hundred articles consistent instead of hand-tidying each one.

Consistency is the real value. Slugs written by hand drift: one post uses underscores, another keeps stop words, a third sneaks in a stray percent sign. Generating them from titles with one fixed rule set keeps a whole site's URLs uniform, which looks professional in search results and avoids encoding surprises in analytics.

How to Use the Slug Generator

Type a title or paste a list with one title per line, choose a separator, and copy the generated slugs. Options cover lowercasing, stripping stop words and limiting length.

  1. Type your page title, or paste a list with one title per line for batch conversion.
  2. Choose a separator: hyphen for URLs, or underscore if a system requires it.
  3. Toggle lowercase and stop-word removal to match your site's convention.
  4. Set a maximum length if you want compact slugs.
  5. Copy a single slug or the whole converted list.
  6. Use the slug as your page's URL path when publishing.

How the Slug Generator Works

Slugification is a chain of small, strict transformations. Accented characters are first transliterated to their base letters, so café becomes cafe and über becomes uber. Every character that is not a letter or digit is replaced by the separator, repeated separators collapse into one, and leading or trailing separators are trimmed. Lowercasing keeps URLs case-insensitive in practice, since some servers treat mixed case as different addresses.

Stop-word removal shortens slugs without losing meaning. Words like a, the, of and to carry little signal in a URL, and removing them turns a-long-guide-to-the-basics-of-baking into long-guide-baking-basics. The option exists because some teams prefer keeping them for readability; the tool supports both conventions and applies whichever you choose consistently across the whole batch.

Batch mode is where the tool earns its keep in real projects. Migrating a site, importing a content export, or pre-generating URLs for a content plan all involve dozens or hundreds of titles. Converting them with one rule set produces uniform slugs that sort sensibly, compare cleanly in analytics, and never contain characters that need percent-encoding. Everything runs locally, so unpublished titles and content plans stay on your machine.

Slugs interact with site architecture more than most people expect. A slug is only the last segment; the full URL also carries category and date segments depending on your permalink structure. Short, keyword-led slugs pay off most on flat structures like example.com/post-slug, while deep structures benefit even more from compact slugs because every extra segment adds length and dilutes the keyword's visual weight in search snippets.

Changing slugs is a normal part of content maintenance, but it must be paired with redirects. A permanent redirect from the old URL transfers most of the accumulated link equity and keeps bookmarks working; skipping it is the single most damaging slug mistake, converting a cosmetic improvement into a traffic loss. Plan slug changes in batches, redirect every old path, and update internal links to point directly at the new addresses.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Leaving spaces or capital letters in URLs by hand, which creates percent-encoded addresses that look messy and split analytics.
  • Mixing separators across a site, some hyphens and some underscores, which breaks consistency and looks unprofessional.
  • Keeping every stop word in long titles, producing URLs that are long without adding meaning.
  • Changing a live page's slug without a redirect, which breaks inbound links and loses search equity.
  • Forgetting that accented titles need transliteration, ending up with percent-encoded characters in the address bar.

Tips, Benefits and Limitations

  • Keep slugs short: three to six meaningful words is a good target for articles.
  • Hyphens are the standard separator for URLs; use underscores only when a specific system demands them.
  • Use batch mode for anything more than a handful of pages so the rule set stays identical.
  • Include the main keyword in the slug naturally, since it appears in results and when people share the bare link.
  • When a slug must change, redirect the old URL to the new one on the same day.
  • Generate slugs from final titles, not working titles, so the URL matches what readers see.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a URL slug?

It is the last segment of a URL identifying a specific page, such as best-coffee-grinders in example.com/blog/best-coffee-grinders, written in lowercase with separators instead of spaces.

Why hyphens instead of underscores?

Search engines have historically treated hyphens as word separators and underscores as word joiners, so hyphens are the established convention for URLs.

Can I convert many titles at once?

Yes. Paste one title per line and the tool returns one slug per line, applying the same options across the whole batch.

Are accented characters handled?

Yes. Characters like é, ü and ñ are transliterated to their base letters before slugifying, producing clean ASCII URLs.

Should I remove stop words from slugs?

Usually yes for shorter, cleaner URLs, but it is a style choice. The tool lets you toggle the option and apply it consistently.

Is anything sent to a server?

No. All conversion happens in your browser, so unpublished titles and content plans never leave your device.
